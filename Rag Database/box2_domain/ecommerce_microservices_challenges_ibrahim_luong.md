## 1 Introduction — Auszug: E-Commerce-spezifische Herausforderungen *(printed p. 477)*

The synthesis of cloud computing and e-commerce has certainly provided businesses with the ability to dynamically access resources, scale operations with ease, and ensure consistent customer experiences across different places. While microservices architecture provides a compelling solution to e-commerce system's scalability and agility concerns, it also brings substantial implementation challenges. These include managing distributed systems, ensuring data consistency, and maintaining security in a cloud-based environment. Given these challenges, the study provides a taxonomy that categorises microservices into separate functional groups, aimed at addressing these challenges in a systematic way.

## 2.2 Monolithic and Microservices Architectures Comparison *(printed p. 478)*

In the e-commerce landscape, choosing between microservices and traditional monolithic architectures requires careful consideration of various factors. Monolithic architectures, characterized by their single, tightly coupled codebase. However, when e-commerce systems increase in size, they frequently face scalability and maintenance issues. Microservices provide an alternative approach by decomposing monolithic applications into smaller, self-contained services [6]. This decomposition results in fine-grained services, each focusing on a specific business capability. Scalability is one of the most major advantages of microservices. E-commerce platforms often experience fluctuating traffic patterns, especially during promotions or seasonal events. Microservices enable organisations to scale individual services, ensuring that resources are deployed efficiently to manage workload properly.

However, microservices introduce complexities related to inter-service communication, data consistency, and debugging in distributed environments [6]. These challenges must be carefully addressed. Furthermore, the shift from a monolithic to a microservices architecture, as supported in the proposed framework by [6], Monolith to Microservices Decomposition Framework (M2MDF), requires a well-structured design and thorough validation in real-world settings. Therefore, the choice between monolithic and microservices architecture in the context of e-commerce should be aligned with organisations' growth path, scalability requirements, and development environment. While microservices offer agility and scalability, monolithic architecture might be suitable for smaller e-commerce businesses with limited resources.

## 2.5 Challenges and Limitations of Microservices in E-commerce *(printed pp. 479–480)*

Despite the advantages of microservices, there are various obstacles and limitations to consider when using them in cloud-based e-commerce systems. A systematic review was conducted by [6], who highlighted numerous important issues and limitation. Firstly, breaking down monolithic applications into microservices can be a time-consuming and resource-intensive operation. Organisations may face challenges in identifying appropriate boundaries for microservices, ensuring proper data consistency, and managing the migration of existing systems. Secondly, because microservices have a distributed architecture, they might increase the complexity of monitoring and debugging. To solve these problems, Abgaz et al. [6] have emphasized the importance of good monitoring and logging system. Moreover, ensuring the security of microservices and their interactions is crucial in e-commerce, where sensitive customer data is often processed. Abgaz et al. [6] have also stated the importance of strong security and identity management inside a microservices ecosystem. Scalability is another challenge, as e-commerce platforms often experience fluctuating traffic loads. According to the research [4], microservices should be designed to scale horizontally to suit increased demand. Furthermore, maintaining data consistency and transactional integrity across several microservices can be difficult. To solve these problems, the findings of [1] suggest investigating event-driven systems and distributed transaction patterns.

In response to the complications of microservices deployment, particularly in cloud-based e-commerce, this study proposes a taxonomy to address these challenges. Microservices, while promising in terms of flexibility and scalability, introduce issues such as service integration, data consistency, and transaction management. Our taxonomy provides a structured approach to these challenges by clearly defining service boundaries, suggesting data management patterns, and proposing robust communication protocols between services, allowing for more efficient implementation and management of microservices architectures.

## 2.6 Previous Works and Taxonomies Related to Microservices — Auszug: unabhängige Skalierung unter Spitzenlast *(printed p. 480)*

The systematic review and the development of the M2MDF give significant insights and direction for organisations attempting to move from monolithic to microservices architectures [6]. This methodology provides an organized way to solve the issues of monolith breakdown, allowing for a more seamless adoption of microservices. However, the M2MDF framework did not adequately address the challenge of scaling microservices independently during high-demand periods in e-commerce, such as during flash sales or promotional events like Black Friday. In addition, the research [5] provides frameworks for containerized microservices orchestration, which align with the broader usage of containerization technologies [5]. These frameworks offer ways for managing microservice execution efficiently, optimising resource utilisation, and reacting to changing workloads [5].
